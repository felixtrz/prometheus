# Open Design Decisions

These questions remain unresolved. They are not commitments to additional features.

## Progression and combat

- What triggers increasing danger: time, exploration, technology, or deliberate milestones? Can players remain in the peaceful stage indefinitely?
- Which combat interactions are supported? How does the automatic crossbow work?
- Do later enemies become dangerous through new behaviors, larger groups, stronger statistics, or a combination?
- What are the technology stages, long-term goals, and conditions for completing the game?

## Crafting and survival detail

- What is the full physical sequence for making stew and a torch? Which repeated steps remain satisfying over many sessions?
- How are recipes discovered, displayed, and followed? What happens when ingredients or actions are incorrect?
- How does hunger affect the player, and how do different foods help?
- How are campfires established, and what happens to dropped items after further deaths?

## World story and presentation

- What happened in the incident, how are clues delivered, and what explains the starting lighter, axe, and tool bench?
- How do generated terrain and authored landmarks connect to the story and progression?
- What sound direction should guide the experience?
- Which specific aspects of *Don’t Starve* should influence progression, survival pressure, world tone, or presentation?
- Finalize the still-open visual decisions listed in [Art Style & Visual Direction](/g61x13g88prmnv/), including palette rules, creature style, UI/HUD language, and final production budgets.

## Platform and production scope

- Confirm persistence between sessions, saving behavior, and how players stop comfortably after 15–30 minutes.
- Choose target headsets, input support, performance requirements, and comfort settings.
- Define the first playable scope: locations, animals, enemies, recipes, equipment, and required story content.
- Set the production constraints: team, engine, budget, schedule, and release scope.
