# World and player journey

## World structure

The world combines hand-designed landmarks with a generated landscape. Exploration leads to new places, materials, ingredients, enemies, and discoveries. The setting, world scale, biome selection, and rules for placing landmarks are still open.

## Story premise

The player cannot remember what happened. Information found in the world reveals details about an incident and provides crafting knowledge, including recipes. The story must also explain why the player has a lighter, an axe, and a tool bench. The nature of the incident, the player’s identity, and the ending remain undecided.

## Reasons to explore

- Find new materials and ingredients for crafting and cooking.
- Hunt animals and fight enemies.
- Discover places and uncover information about the incident.
- Find crafting recipes and other useful knowledge.

## Home and respawning

Home is a campfire. Sleeping at a campfire makes it the player’s respawn point; the last campfire slept at is the active one. Sleeping therefore has a checkpoint role even though fatigue has not been selected as a survival need. Permanent bases, additional buildings, and campfire construction rules are not yet defined.

## Survival and death

Hunger is the selected need that players actively manage. Cooking supports the survival experience, with meat stew as a concrete example. Hunger depletion, food benefits, and the consequences of starvation remain open.

On death, the player respawns at the last campfire they slept at. Carried belongings drop at the death location and can be recovered. Rules for repeated deaths, item despawning, and respawning before the first campfire sleep remain open.

## Proposed core loop

Explore and gather or hunt → bring materials together → physically craft tools and cook food → use improved equipment to explore further and uncover recipes and story clues → sleep at a campfire to set a respawn point. This sequence synthesizes the established systems; its pacing and exact order still need validation.
